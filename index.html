ABOUT THIS PROJECT

What am I looking at?

This map predicts block-level surface temperature across Lahore and Karachi — how hot the ground itself gets, not the air — using a machine learning model trained on satellite and street-level data.

How it works

Each city is divided into a grid of 500m × 500m blocks. For every block, four features are measured from public satellite imagery and map data:

  • NDBI — how built-up / concrete-covered the block is
  • NDVI — how much vegetation is present
  • Elevation — height above sea level
  • Distance to coast & road density — geography and infrastructure

A Random Forest model was trained on real Landsat 8 thermal imagery to learn how these features predict surface temperature — reaching 87–89% accuracy (R²) on unseen blocks.

What it found

The two cities turned out to be hot for different reasons. In Lahore, an inland city, concrete and built-up density is by far the strongest driver of heat. In Karachi, a coastal city, elevation and distance from the sea dominate instead — the ocean's cooling effect outweighs land-use patterns.

Try it

Click any colored block on the map to see a plain-language breakdown of why that specific spot is hot or cool, based on how its features compare to the city average.
